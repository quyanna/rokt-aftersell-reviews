Support triage sheet - Aftersell by Rokt

What would land in my queue, and what would stop it landing there

Every public App Store review for Aftersell and UpCart, read and sorted into the tickets they would have become. For each recurring type: what the merchant says, what is actually going on, who can resolve it, whether the answer is already written down, and a first reply worth sending.

1,765 reviews 2020-02-07 to 2026-08-26 122 contain a complaint 12 recurring types Built 26 Aug 2026
01

Read this first

This measures loud complaints, not common ones. Treating it as a picture of the real queue would be wrong in a specific and predictable direction.

The limitation that matters most

People who quietly uninstall never write a review. Neither do the people whose problem was solved in four minutes. A public review is written by someone angry enough, or delighted enough, to stop and type. Everything here is drawn from that population.

A real support queue would skew far more mundane than this document: password resets, where-is-this-setting, one-line billing questions that never reach a review page. Nothing in this dataset can size those, so nothing here tries to.

Three more things to know before trusting a number

122 complaints is too few for statistics. Every figure here is a count, described as a pattern. No percentages are presented as findings, no trend lines are drawn, and where a category has three reviews it says three.

Resolved problems that were not named are not counted. A merchant writing "had some issues but support fixed it in ten minutes" generated a real ticket. It is recorded here as no complaint, because there is no ticket type in it. This document measures identifiable ticket types, not ticket volume, and the gap between those two is larger than the counts suggest.

206 reviews (12%) have a star rating and no text at all. They are excluded from every count below, because there is nothing in them to read.

What this document is for

Not market research, and not an assessment of the product. It is a working answer to one question: if I were answering these tickets, what would keep arriving, what could I actually do about it, and what would stop it arriving at all.

11

What the split says about the job

Of 122 complaints, 109 could be assigned an owner from the review text and 13 could not.

39 Support resolves it Settings, CSS, a refund, or showing where something lives.
39 Support explains it Nothing is broken. A Shopify rule, the pricing model, or a feature that does not exist.
31 Support triages, engineering fixes Support still takes first contact and reproduces it.

Roughly two thirds of what arrives can be closed by the person who reads it. That is the shape of the job.

Where this number is weaker than it looks

It is not a measurement. Whether a support agent may issue a particular refund, or write custom CSS for a merchant, is a matter of internal policy that appears nowhere in the reviews, the documentation, or the App Store listing. Every assignment here is what a careful reader concluded from what the merchant wrote.

The dataset does establish some of it. 65 reviews describe support writing custom CSS, so theme work really is in scope. Merchants describe receiving refunds. The documentation settles whether a feature exists. Beyond those, the assignment is inference.

The third label was originally "needs engineering". It was reworded after the hand-audit pointed out that support takes first contact on every ticket regardless of category, so a label implying otherwise made the figure read as a hand-off rate.

12

When complaints arrive

The brief expected a cluster in week one, which would point at onboarding. The data says the opposite.

Complaint rate roughly triples across the range, from 3.9% of first-day reviews to 11.7% at one to two years. Early reviewers are overwhelmingly positive. In the first month, 50 of 923 reviews contain a complaint; past a year it is 19 of 204.

The thing to notice

439 reviews were written by merchants who had used the app for less than a day, and they are the most positive group in the dataset. A quarter of all reviews here were written before the merchant could plausibly have seen a result. That inflates the overall rating and it means early enthusiasm is not evidence the product worked.

The type of complaint changes too

In the first month it is theme conflicts, reliability and requests for features. Past a year it is billing and support quality. Those need different fixes: the first group is a setup problem, the second is trust wearing through.

Aftersell's complaints are about money and people. Billing surprises and support experience lead, alongside feature requests from merchants who are otherwise happy.

Why this is good news for support

Theme conflicts are the most fixable category in the whole dataset: 11 of 13 were resolvable by support directly, usually with CSS written in the conversation. UpCart's worse rating is driven substantially by a problem support is well placed to solve, rather than by anything that needs engineering.

15

Checking the documentation findings against the vendor

Aftersell publishes a documentation assistant that searches the actual page content. The findings above were judged from page titles and descriptions only, so the assistant was asked the same questions a merchant would.

Four tests. 2 confirmed a finding. 2 corrected one. The corrections are shown because they are the more useful half.

How do I submit a feature request?

Relates to: Wants something the app does not do

The docs don't cover a formal feature request process. For that, I'd recommend contacting support directly. You can send feature suggestions to support@aftersell.app, the docs don't describe a separate feature-request form.

Confirmed

Backs the claim that no feature request process is documented. The vendor's own assistant, searching actual page content rather than the index, reached the same answer this analysis did from titles alone.

What are your support hours and response times?

Relates to: Could not get help, or the help did not help

I couldn't find any documentation on specific support hours or response time commitments. The docs don't list specific support hours or SLA response times. You can reach the Aftersell team via the in-app chat, or email support@aftersell.app.

Confirmed

Backs the claim and sharpens it. The gap is not that contacting support is undocumented, since the answer to that is simply to contact support. The gap is that nothing sets an expectation about how long a reply takes, which is exactly what the 13 merchants in this category were angry about.

A customer was charged for an upsell they declined. What do I do?

Relates to: The app changed a customer's order

The docs don't directly cover a 'clicked decline but got charged anyway' scenario. What you may be seeing is likely a Partially Paid Order, or a double-charge appearance on Replacement Upsells. Check the order timeline in Shopify Admin. If neither matches, this may need direct investigation from support.

Corrected the finding

Corrects the claim. This analysis called the category undocumented; the assistant surfaced three adjacent pages the title-only method missed. The tag is corrected to documented but buried. The specific scenario merchants describe is still not covered, and the assistant says so.

UpCart added a duplicate free gift to an order. How do I fix it?

Relates to: The app changed a customer's order

For an already-placed order with a duplicate free gift, the docs don't cover editing completed orders directly. To prevent it going forward, see Preventing reward exploits. For the existing order itself, you'd need to edit or refund it directly in Shopify admin, the docs don't detail order editing, so contact support for help there.

Corrected the finding

The sharpest documentation finding in the project. The docs cover PREVENTION and not REMEDIATION. A page exists explaining how to stop duplicate rewards happening. Nothing explains what to do about the order that already has one. Every merchant who writes in has the second problem, not the first.

The pattern worth taking away

The documentation covers prevention and not remediation. There is a page explaining how to stop customers claiming a reward twice. There is nothing explaining what to do about the order that already has one. Every merchant who writes in has the second problem, not the first.

The same shape appears elsewhere. Pricing is documented, disputed charges are not. Theme compatibility is documented, a store already broken by a conflict is not. A support queue is made almost entirely of people whose problem has already happened.

A note on the reward page

The mitigation for double-claimed rewards is real and documented, and it is opt-in. Until a merchant finds a page titled "Preventing reward exploits" and follows it, the default configuration allows a customer to add a reward product from its own product page and claim it twice. That is a setup default worth raising at onboarding rather than leaving for the merchant to discover after losing money on it.

The assistant is a semantic search over the docs, so it may find a page a merchant using site search or Google would miss. That makes it strong evidence when it fails to find something and weaker evidence when it succeeds.

16

How this was built, and how it was checked

Claude read and categorised the reviews. That is only worth something if the output was checked, so here is what was checked and what was not.

The pipeline

  1. Every review page for both apps was downloaded and saved. Shopify's robots.txt permits this path; requests were made one second apart with a self-identifying user agent.
  2. The saved pages were parsed into a SQLite database, one row per review. The parser checks its own output before it will report success.
  3. Each of the 1,559 reviews with text was sent to Claude for categorisation, with the answer cached so nothing is ever re-read.
  4. Each ticket type was checked against Aftersell's published documentation index of 265 pages.
  5. This document was generated from the database. No figure in it was typed by hand.

What was verified mechanically

Every decided judgement had to quote the exact words from the review that justify it. A script string-matches every quote and every extracted name against its source review, and fails if one does not appear.

Passing

609 of 609 staff names appear in the review that names them.
109 of 109 evidence quotes are literal spans of their review.

What that proves

That nothing was invented. It does not prove the judgements are correct, and no automated check could.

Checked by hand

41 classified reviews were read by a person on 2026-08-26, with 1 disagreement. The sample was drawn at random across every ticket type, and each entry showed the review, the decision, the reasoning, and the exact words from the review said to justify it.

The disagreement changed this document. A review labelled "needs engineering" was challenged on the grounds that support could reproduce and explain the issue first, escalating only if necessary. That was right about all 31 reviews carrying that label, not just the one, so the label is now worded "support triages, engineering fixes".

The limit of that check: the reader had the same review text the model had, and no access to internal policy. Two readers agreeing is consistency, not correctness.

What is still unverified

Whether a support agent is permitted to issue a given refund. Whether a specific charge was correct, which needs the merchant's invoice. Whether a reported fault is a defect or a misconfiguration, which needs someone to reproduce it. Roughly half the complaints turn on one of those, and the honest position is that they are questions rather than findings.

The questions this raises

  • Nothing in 265 documentation pages explains how to reach a human, what response times to expect, or how to escalate. Is that deliberate?
  • Nothing covers products appearing on a customer's order without consent, which six merchants describe. What does that ticket do today?
  • The analytics and Klaviyo interaction is documented, but under titles nobody would search. Who owns renaming a page?
  • How much of a refund can support issue without approval? That single answer moves a large share of the billing tickets between two columns.
